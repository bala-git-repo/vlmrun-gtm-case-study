Part A · The list

A1Find

  • Started from VLM Run's 4 real customers: small vertical software companies whose product eats messy paperwork.
  • 6 Blitz search recipes, tightened from v1 to v4 by hand-checking samples. Software-only industries and a competitor blocklist did most of the cleanup.
  • Batch 2: lookalikes of our top scorers plus 6 more countries, which raised the #100 score from 42 to 51 with competitors cut.

A2Enrich

  • Free first: 565 → 232 (Blitz about text) → 194 (their own website).
  • Kept for each company: visual core, docs / images / video, a word-for-word quote + URL, and regulated-data and deployment flags.
  • Paid last, survivors only: ML/AI hiring and engineering headcount.

A3Score

  • Fit score out of 90, evidence only. 70 for the need (product runs on documents/photos/video, messy input, video, already extracting today) and 20 for the buyer (sensitive data, right size).
  • Minus points for building their own vision models, being a services firm, or being owned by a bigger company.
  • Act now is a separate flag (AI hiring, a new round, fast growth, a new tech leader, a recent post about document pain). It never adds points, so a recent hire can't beat a better fit. 38 of the hundred have it.
  • Unreadable website = "not enough data", set aside, not scored as zero. We re-opened all 33 in a browser: 24 re-scored on what the live site says, 9 set aside.
  • Checked three ways: real customers land ~#7, ~#8 and ~#12 (Scan.com ~#141 is the miss) · moving any weight ±20% keeps 95+ of the top 100 · 10 rows checked by hand → 4 rule fixes.

A4The hundred

  • #1 Avallon AI 85 → #100 Pibit.AI 51 (out of 90). 71 of 100 score 60+.
  • Competitor check: 29 companies that sell extraction or vision AI themselves were cut, and companies that built their own OCR or vision models take −15 (e.g. Coral, #48). Every account left reads messy docs, photos or video inside its own product and doesn't sell that as the product.
  • Every row: scores, play, who to contact, a verified quote. In the Sheet, tab Top 100.

A5Segments

  • A · Regulated doc intake (58): lead with accuracy plus zero retention, BAA and VPC.
  • B · Field & freight (30): lead with docs and photos in one API.
  • C · Video-first (12): lead with video into JSON.
  • Leave out anyone who sells extraction or vision AI, and anyone who built their own VLM.

A6Scale

  • ~2,300 companies match our tight searches, and we pulled ~1,000. About 1 in 10 makes the top grade.
  • ~19 credits per top-100 account. Covering the rest costs ≈2,600 credits, so we stopped under our cap.
  • What breaks: keyword splits, docs wrong on cost, fake evidence, stale signals.

Part B · The outreach

B1Signals

  • Company: hiring ML/doc-AI engineers, job posts naming Textract or Tesseract, a new intake product page, VLM Run signups.
  • People: a new CTO or Head of AI, a customer champion who moved, someone posting about OCR pain.
  • A strong signal sends the signal email within 48h. Signals change timing, not targeting.
  • Checked live with Crustdata: 34 of the top 100 have a signal now: 8 are hiring engineers for OCR or document extraction, 18 posted about document pain (e.g. Luma Health on fax volume), Transflo has a new SVP of AI, and Pace, Outmarket, Digs and Lama AI raised recently. Together with AI hiring on their own sites, 38 are flagged act now. The posts also catch fit problems: Coral and iCustoms built their own OCR/IDP, so they take −15. Full table in the sheet tab Crustdata enrichment.

B2People

  • 30 / 40 / 40 titles → Blitz people search → waterfall (decision maker > influencer > champion) → email finders → MillionVerifier → BounceBan for catch-alls → one sendable.csv.
  • No email found → LinkedIn only.
  • Tested on the top 100: Crustdata found tech decision makers or founders at 92 of 100 (163 people, names and LinkedIn only, nobody contacted).

B3Copy

  • 3 emails + a signal version per play. 25–55 words, one fact, an offer to run their samples. See the emails →
  • LinkedIn: founder and growth accounts connect the same day, then wait 30–60 days before one message. Lowest-scored accounts go first.
